# Architecture, space and structure

"Architecture" here means three things that feed each other:

1. **Built architecture** — spatial references, facades, interiors, material,
   light, scale.
2. **Information architecture** — how a system is organized, named and
   navigated; applicable to a portfolio, a design system or a composition.
3. **Visual architecture** — grid, hierarchy, layers, rhythm, structure of a
   piece.

### How to look at architecture for wallpaper design

- **Light**: look for direction and contrast. A hard noon facade and a blue-hour
  facade tell different stories.
- **Geometry**: repetition, window rhythm, diagonals, curves. The camera can
  turn a facade into an abstract pattern.
- **Human scale**: a small figure provides scale and narrative; it also gives a
  focal point to preserve when cropping.
- **Materiality**: concrete, brick, metal, wood, plaster. Grain and texture
  survive blur and grain; they are wallpaper allies.
- **Perspective**: converging lines guide the eye. On desktop, direct them
  toward the center or the third where you want the focus.
- **Negative space**: sky, a blank wall or a shadow give breathing room and room
  for icons. Do not fill everything.

### Minimum checklist

1. **Inventory**: list everything before ordering it.
2. **Mental model**: use the user's words, not the database's.
3. **Hierarchy**: maximum three levels before people get lost.
4. **Navigation**: always visible, predictable, always with an exit.
5. **Search**: if there are more than about 50 items, it needs search.
6. **Labels**: consistent, short, specific.
7. **URLs**: readable, stable, no cryptic IDs.
8. **Metadata**: title, description, license, author, date.
9. **Governance**: who adds, who archives, by what rule.
10. **Test**: ask someone to find something without explaining your system.

## Visual architecture of a piece

- **Grid**: 12 columns for layout; 3x3 for photographic composition.
- **Hierarchy**: one focus, one secondary, one background. If everything
  shouts, nothing shouts.
- **Layers**: background (color/image), field (blur/texture), subject (sharp),
  accents (type/line), grain (unifier).
- **Rhythm**: repeat an interval (8/16/24/40) and break it once.
- **Alignment**: an invisible master line holds everything.
- **Contrast**: light/dark, full/empty, straight/curved, dense/light.
- **Scale**: relative size creates drama; do not use one weight for everything.
