# Inspiration and reference

Inspiration is not copying: it is identifying the **mechanism** (composition,
light, typography, rhythm, material) and reusing it with different content.

## Building a useful moodboard

1. **Define the intent** in one sentence: "monumental calm", "warm technology",
   "domestic brutalism".
2. **Separate layers**: color, typography, composition, texture, light,
   architectural reference.
3. **Include opposites**: what yes and what no. The "no" defines as much as the
   "yes".
4. **Annotate the mechanism**: "three-stop gradients with grain", "giant serif
   headline over desaturated photo", "12-column grid with one empty column".
5. **Reduce to 9-12 images**. More is noise.
6. **Test in the final format**: 16:9, 9:20, print, thumbnail.
7. **Extract a palette** and use it as a constraint.
8. **Close the reference** when you start producing; design from the system,
   not from the anxiety of browsing.

## Anti-patterns

- Copying an exact composition without changing the content.
- Mixing five styles because you like all of them.
- Using a web reference for a wallpaper without adapting the scale.
- Confusing "minimalist" with "empty without hierarchy".
- Adding grain/blur to hide a weak composition. The effect does not fix the
  structure.
- Chasing trends without asking what the piece needs.
